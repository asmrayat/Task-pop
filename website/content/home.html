A quick list that shows up on its own.

No app to remember to open. TaskPop lives on your computer, appears the moment you start using it, and comes back with a double-tap from whatever you’re doing.

  • Pops up when you start your computer

    Open the lid or wake your computer and your tasks slide in from the side. It can also appear when you unlock the screen, or stay quiet until you call it.

    When I start my computer
    When I unlock the screen

  • Double-tap to open it, from any app

    Tap Control twice and your list slides in, on whichever desktop you’re on and over full-screen apps too. Tap twice again and it’s gone. You can pick Option, Command or Shift instead (Alt or Shift on Windows), or use ⌃⌥T or a shortcut of your own.

    ⌃control ×2
    or press ⌃⌥T in any app

  • Categories keep things apart

    Small tags along the top: All, Personal, Work and any you add. Click one to see only its tasks, and whatever you add there goes into it. Drag a task onto a tag, or type #work as you add it.

    All 6 Personal 3 Work 2 Study 1 +
    Finish the Q3 deck Work

  • Important tasks stay on top

    Star a task, or start it with an exclamation mark, and it sits above everything else until it’s done.

    Pay the electricity bill
    !Call the bank before 5

  • A timer for what can’t wait

    Give a task 15 minutes, an afternoon or a day. It jumps to the top with a countdown and a line that shrinks as the time runs out, turning orange near the end. A “Time’s up” notification arrives right on time.

Click Show, confirm with Touch ID or your password (Windows Hello on a PC), then copy all of it, one line, or just the part you select.

    Right-click a task to be reminded in an hour, this evening, tomorrow morning, or at any time you choose. Mark it done or snooze it straight from the notification.

    Click the … on a task and TaskPop reads dates like “tomorrow at 3pm” from it. Set the time, length, place or a Meet link, and Google Calendar opens in your browser with the event ready to save. Nothing is connected to your Google account.

    Set a task to repeat every day and it un-ticks itself overnight, ready for tomorrow. Its reminder comes back too.

  • Fast with the keyboard

    Arrows to move, Space to tick, Return to edit, T for a timer, ← and → to switch categories, ⌘J for the note and ⌘Z to undo (Ctrl on Windows). Drag tasks to reorder them, or move them with ⌥↑ and ⌥↓.

Your tasks stay on your computer.

There’s no account to create and nothing is sent to a server. TaskPop saves every change on your computer, keeps a backup of the previous save, and lets you export your list whenever you want. Text you hide in the quick note is encrypted with a key that macOS keeps in your keychain, or Windows keeps for your account, and TaskPop only ever learns whether Touch ID or Windows Hello said yes.

Installed in about a minute.

  1. Download the installer

    Choose macOS or Windows below. The Mac file is one installer for Apple Silicon and Intel.

  2. Open the installer

    On a Mac, open the disk image and double-click Install TaskPop. On Windows, run the .exe: it installs just for you, with no administrator prompt.

  3. Allow it once

    On a Mac, open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down, click Open Anyway and confirm. If Windows shows SmartScreen, choose More info, then Run anyway.

  4. Take the one-minute tour

    TaskPop opens with a short tour: pick the key you’ll double-tap and try it on a big on-screen key. On a Mac it shows you where to allow it, too.

Questions

Why does macOS ask me to confirm the installer?

TaskPop isn’t distributed through the Mac App Store, so macOS treats it as an app from an unidentified developer. After you open the installer, go to System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down and click Open Anyway. Confirm the prompt. You only need to do this once.

Which computers does TaskPop work on?

Any Mac running macOS 13 Ventura or later — Apple Silicon and Intel use the same download — and PCs running Windows 10 or 11, x64 or ARM64.

Where are my tasks saved?

Only on the computer you use: on a Mac in ~/Library/Application Support/TaskPop, on Windows in %APPDATA%\TaskPop. Every change is saved straight away with a backup of the previous version, and you can export your tasks and categories to a file from Settings.

Can I change the double-tap key or the shortcut?

Yes. In Settings → General, choose the key you double-tap (Control, Option, Command or Shift on a Mac; Ctrl, Alt or Shift on Windows) or turn the double-tap off. Click the keyboard shortcut to record a different one. Only a quick tap on its own counts, so shortcuts like ⌘C or Ctrl+C never open the panel.

Why does my Mac ask for Input Monitoring?

That’s what lets the double-tap work while you’re in another app. TaskPop only reads whether keys like Control are held down, plus a count of other key presses so shortcuts don’t count as taps. It never records what you type. If the double-tap stops working after an update, open System Settings → Privacy & Security → Input Monitoring, remove TaskPop with −, add it back with + and restart TaskPop. The keyboard shortcut and the menu bar icon work without this permission.

How private is hidden text in the quick note?

Hidden text is encrypted the moment you hide it, with a key that macOS keeps in your keychain, or Windows keeps for your account. It’s never saved, backed up or copied as plain text. To show or copy it, TaskPop asks your Mac for Touch ID or your password (Windows Hello or your Windows password on a PC) and is only told yes or no. While it shows you can select any part of it, so an email and the password under it can be copied separately. It hides itself again after a minute, and copied text is taken off the clipboard after a minute too. For a password, click Add hidden to type it straight into a password box.

How do categories and timers work?

Categories are the small tags at the top of the panel. Click one to see only its tasks, drag a task onto a tag to move it, or type #work when you add it. Timers give a task a time frame: click … → Set a timer, or select the task and press T. It moves to the top with a countdown, and you get a “Time’s up” notification with Mark as done and Add 15 min.

Will it pop up every time I unlock my computer?

By default it appears when you start your computer and when you unlock the screen. You can turn either of these off in Settings.

Does it sync between computers?

No, your tasks stay on the computer you use. To move them to another computer, export them from Settings and import the file there.

Does it work on Windows?

Yes. Download the Windows installer, run the .exe, and follow the setup steps. If Windows shows a SmartScreen warning, choose More info and then Run anyway.

How do I add a task to Google Calendar?

Click the … on the task, choose a date and time, and TaskPop opens Google Calendar in your browser with the event filled in. Save it there if you want it on your calendar. The task itself stays on your computer.

How do I update TaskPop?

TaskPop checks for a new version on its own, shortly after you open it and again every few hours. When one is out, a small “New update is here” pop-up shows what’s new: choose Update now, or Update later to be asked again in a few hours. TaskPop checks the download against its published checksum, installs it and reopens. On a Mac you enter your password once; on Windows there are no extra prompts. Your tasks and settings stay. If your copy is older and has no updater, download the newest installer from this page once.

How do I uninstall it?

On a Mac, click the TaskPop icon in the menu bar, turn off Open at Login and choose Quit. Then drag TaskPop from Applications to the Trash. To remove your tasks too, delete the TaskPop folder in ~/Library/Application Support. On Windows, open Settings → Apps → Installed apps, find TaskPop and choose Uninstall; it asks whether to delete your tasks and settings as well.

How to install TaskPop on macOS

macOS will show a security warning because TaskPop is not from the App Store. Follow these steps once.

  1. Open the downloaded file Find the TaskPop disk image in Downloads and double-click it. Then double-click Install TaskPop.
  2. If you see “Apple cannot check it for malicious software” Click OK or Done on that warning. This is normal for apps that are not in the Mac App Store.
  3. Open Privacy & Security Go to the Apple menu → System Settings → Privacy & Security. Scroll to the Security section at the bottom.
  4. Click Open Anyway You should see a note that TaskPop was blocked. Click Open Anyway, enter your password or use Touch ID, then click Open.
  5. Finish the installer Follow the remaining installer screens. TaskPop opens in the menu bar with a one-minute tour. Then double-tap control or press ⌃⌥T any time.
